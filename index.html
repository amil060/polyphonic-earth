A jukebox that turns NASA's pictures of Earth into music, so anyone, including people who are blind, can explore our changing planet by ear.

      How the sonification works

      Each frame of the NASA visualization is drawn into a hidden canvas and analysed pixel by pixel (192 × 96 grid). The pixel colours are converted back into data values using the visualization's own colour scale. Before the music starts, a sound legend plays the lowest and the highest value once, like the axis of a chart. A slow sweep then moves from west to east once per bar. Each of the 8 slices of the map becomes a note: its value sets the pitch, how much of it contains data sets the loudness, and its position sets the stereo pan. The layers enter one at a time, and you can switch each one on or off. Notes use quarter-tone maqam scales (Rast, Saba, Hijaz): the value picks a scale degree and what is left over bends the note by up to 25 cents, so small differences in the data stay audible. In the CO₂ scene the chord's two voices drift apart in cents as concentration rises, and you hear them beat. A soft knock marks a slice with no data, for example land in the ocean scene.
